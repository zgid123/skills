# Synthetic draft and evidence appendix

This is invented evaluation material, not a real manuscript or publication. Cite E1-E3 as supplied evidence notes without inventing authors, publication dates or external references. The draft contains deliberately unsupported claims to be revised.

## Draft

Our randomized controlled trial proves that the scheduling assistant causes a 30% improvement in task completion time for all remote workers. Eighteen volunteers completed their usual scheduling tasks before and after using the assistant. Each individual improved, and the results were statistically significant. The assistant eliminates privacy concerns because it uses no sensitive data. A larger replication confirms that these benefits persist over time. We recommend immediate deployment in all workplaces.

## E1: Observation note

Eighteen volunteers from one remote team were recruited by convenience sampling. Everyone received the assistant. The observation lasted two weeks. There was no comparison group, randomization or masking. Aggregate median scheduling time was 42 minutes before adoption and 36 minutes after adoption. Participant-level timings, dispersion and paired differences were not retained. No inferential statistical test was performed.

## E2: Feedback note

Six of the 18 participants requested clearer information about how long scheduling data would be retained. No privacy audit or review of sensitive-data handling was performed. No satisfaction score or other numerical feedback result was collected.

## E3: Planned work

A larger replication with another team and a longer observation period has been proposed but not started. The team has not selected its recruitment method or analysis plan. Task mix and learning effects were not controlled in the two-week observation.
