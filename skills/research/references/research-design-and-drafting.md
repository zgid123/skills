# Research design and drafting

## Develop a feasible study

Start from the research question and evidence-backed gap. Establish the intended contribution and study type using the discipline and supplied context. Propose the smallest design that can answer the question credibly.

Connect each question to its proposed method, data or participants, measures or analytic approach, comparison or validation strategy, and criteria for interpreting results. Address resources, feasibility, ethics, privacy, and reproducibility where relevant. Justify design choices using eligible evidence; identify choices that remain untested assumptions.

For an empirical proposal, describe planned data collection, analysis, and evaluation in future or conditional language. For conceptual, theoretical, qualitative, or design research, use suitable forms of argument and validation rather than forcing an experimental paper. Label sample-size rationales, hypotheses, and anticipated outcomes as proposals. Do not fabricate approvals, preregistration, collected data, or completed analyses.

If actual data analysis is requested, optionally use Data Analyst when installed. Otherwise analyze the supplied data with available tools, document assumptions and data handling, and verify calculations against inputs. Return to the manuscript only with verified results and their limits; if analysis could not be executed, retain clearly marked missing results.

## Build an argument from evidence

Documents and its paper-writing guidance are optional when installed. Independently, follow the supplied template and existing manuscript conventions, write clear prose suited to the audience, and preserve the selected citation style. Research notes and requested prose drafts can remain Markdown. For requested manuscript files, use available production tools, deliver editable source, and verify the compiled or rendered output; report missing artifacts or checks if tools are unavailable. Missing Documents does not block drafting or require a new formatting system.

1. Outline the problem, evidence, gap, research question, contribution, methods, and implications appropriate to the document stage. For revisions, inspect the existing argument and preserve unrelated material.
2. Map substantive claims to eligible sources and planned or observed study evidence. Reuse traceable evidence notes, then target additional reading or discovery at unsupported claims and contradictions. Distinguish literature findings from the researcher's own findings.
3. Draft only what the available evidence supports. Use clearly marked placeholders for missing results or citation details. If a completed paper is requested without results, deliver supported sections and identify the missing evidence; do not turn expected improvements into observed performance.
4. Attach citations to the claims they support. Verify bibliographic fields and source locations; generate bibliography entries only from supplied or checked metadata. For literature claims supported only by a secondary source, disclose the indirect attribution rather than implying the original was inspected.
5. Apply [Source policy](source-policy.md) to new references and substantively reviewed existing citations. Identify claims relying on excluded sources, then replace or revise only with verified eligible support. Report unresolved dependencies.
6. Check the final argument for alignment among question, contribution, method, results, discussion, abstract, and conclusion. Remove unsupported causal language, universal novelty, and inflated generalization. Count requested word limits after revisions using the user's inclusion rules.

## Revision and submission support

For reviewer comments, create a response matrix mapping each point to the action taken, manuscript location, and remaining limitation. State whether changes were actually made or merely proposed. Never claim to have performed an additional experiment in a response letter unless it was performed.

For submission preparation, establish the target venue and submission stage and verify current official requirements using available tools; optionally use installed document style guidance. Source eligibility and manuscript appearance are separate: the IEEE Access blacklist does not prohibit IEEE citation style or other IEEE venues. Do not claim compliance, successful compilation, visual inspection, or submission unless verified. Submission itself requires explicit user instruction.
