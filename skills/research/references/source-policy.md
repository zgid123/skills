# Source policy

Apply this policy before admitting papers to the research corpus, including user-supplied papers. Keep screening decisions separate from the accepted evidence so exclusions remain auditable without being cited as support.

## Blacklist

| Venue       | Decision | Reason                                    |
| ----------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| IEEE Access | Exclude  | Explicit user source-selection preference |

Exclude IEEE Access papers from recommended reading, substantive paper summaries, evidence synthesis, gap justification, and manuscript citations. A request to summarize a blacklisted paper should receive a brief eligibility explanation and an offer to find eligible alternatives; metadata inspection for screening is allowed. An explicit later user instruction changing this policy can override it for the stated task; merely supplying a paper or asking to use it does not silently remove the blacklist.

Match the publication venue, normalizing capitalization and surrounding or repeated whitespace. Do not infer venue from an IEEE domain, an IEEE DOI prefix, the word “Access” in a title, or a mention of IEEE Access in another paper's references. Other IEEE journals and conferences remain eligible under the project's other criteria. IEEE citation style is unaffected.

Verify venue using the publisher record, reliable bibliographic metadata, or the supplied paper's publication information. If evidence conflicts or the venue is unknown, mark the paper pending and keep it out of recommendations, synthesis, and new citations until resolved. Record the uncertainty and available verification route rather than pretending verification succeeded.

A repository copy, author manuscript, or preprint known to represent the same IEEE Access publication remains excluded. Link versions using reliable metadata or an explicit version relationship; a similar title alone does not establish identity. A separate eligible publication is not automatically excluded because its authors also published in IEEE Access.

Record exclusions with paper identity, venue, decision, and reason. If the blacklist removes evidence central to a question, disclose the coverage limitation and search for alternatives. Do not silently relax the rule or claim excluded work never existed.

## Other source eligibility

- Allow peer-reviewed papers, preprints, theses, technical reports, and supporting materials when relevant, labeling each accurately. Do not treat a preprint as peer reviewed because its authors intend to submit it.
- Establish project-specific inclusion criteria when needed: question fit, population or setting, methods, language, date range, publication type, and evidence access. Do not invent arbitrary date or ranking cutoffs.
- Prefer primary research for claims about a study's methods and results. Reviews can map a field and suggest candidate primary sources; do not claim to have read the original studies through a review.
- Check accessible publisher notices or metadata for corrections, withdrawal, or retraction when verifying papers. Exclude retracted work from supporting evidence; it may be identified as retracted when the research question specifically concerns it. Apply corrections to the claims they affect. If checks are unavailable, say so.
- Use lawful full-text access through supplied files, publisher pages, repositories, or author manuscripts. If full text is inaccessible, report the limit and use abstract-only evidence only for claims that the abstract actually supports.

## Existing manuscripts

During substantive review, identify IEEE Access citations and the claims depending on them. Replace support only after finding and checking an eligible source; otherwise mark the claim as unsupported or revise it to match available evidence. Do not swap a citation while preserving an unsupported assertion. Retain an audit note explaining exclusions and changes.

During formatting-only work, preserve existing text and references; Documents is optional when installed. Do not turn layout work into a literature review or delete blacklisted entries without a substantive editing request.
